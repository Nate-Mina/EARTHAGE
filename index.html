Pillar I: Physical Anomalies

The Zircon Helium "Leaky Clock"

Zircon crystals from the Fenton Hill (NM) borehole contain helium produced by radioactive decay. Because helium is a small, noble gas, it leaks out of crystals rapidly. If the Earth were billions of years old, there should be zero helium left in these crystals.

The 58% Paradox

Samples at 197°C retain 58% of their helium. Based on the measured diffusion rate, this retention is physically impossible if the crystals are 1.5 billion years old. It is, however, perfectly consistent with a 6,000-year history.

Pillar IV: The Chronometric Critique

Why Radiometric Dating Fails

The "millions of years" conclusion relies on the **Invariance Assumption**—the idea that environment has no effect on decay. However, isotope systems are frequently discordant (giving different answers for the same rock).

Isotopic Discordance (Evidence)

The RATE project tested 10 different Grand Canyon rock samples. In almost every case, different isotopic "clocks" yielded wildly different ages for the exact same rock.

Rock Unit Method A Method B Discrepancy
Cardenas Basalt 1,111 My (Rb-Sr) 516 My (K-Ar) 595 Million Years
Brahma Schist 1,721 My (Pb-Pb) 1,240 My (Sm-Nd) 481 Million Years
Bass Rapids Diabase 1,375 My (Sm-Nd) 841 My (K-Ar) 534 Million Years

If dating were "proven fact," these methods should converge on the same number. They do not.

The Carbon Reservoir Illusion

Modern Carbon dating assumes the ratio of C-14 to C-12 has always been the same. But the flood drastically changed two variables:

  • 01. Biosphere Dilution: A massive pre-flood vegetation pool (now coal/oil) would dilute the C-14 ratio, making ancient samples look 10x older.
  • 02. Magnetic Field: A stronger pre-flood magnetic field would shield the atmosphere from C-14 production.

The Diamond Test

C-14 has a half-life of 5,730 years. It should be undetectable after 100,000 years. Yet, C-14 is consistently found in diamonds that are claimed to be 1-3 billion years old.
